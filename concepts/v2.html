Neoclassical × Bauhaus, five readable variations

Same language, same paper and primaries. What changes is how much Neoclassical detailing survives on a phone: the current build leans on small caps, hairlines and a Didone serif at text sizes, which are print idioms. Every variation below applies the readable type scale first, then chooses a different structure for navigation and lists. Left: phone. Right: the same screen on a tablet or desktop.

The type scale every variation shares
BodyJost 16 px, line height 1.45. Currently 15.5 px with 10–11 px labels in many places.
Labels / eyebrowsJost 600 at 12 px, tracking .08em. Currently .6–.68 rem with .24–.30em tracking, which reads as decoration, not text.
SerifBodoni Moda only at 28 px and above (page titles, ledger numerals). Never for tile titles or hints. Hairline serifs vanish below that.
Keycaps12 px Jost 600, tracking .04em, 8 px padding. Currently 10 px.
RulesOne weight of ink rule per level. No shared-border lattices of 1 px lines inside cards on phones.
Glyph names11 px Jost. Currently 8 px uppercase, which is illegible.
